### W01 foundation notes for the next developer

`Transaction` records account movement; `TransactionSplit` records budget attribution. Transfers currently cannot be split across budget lines. Add a distinct jar-allocation model rather than allowing transfers to masquerade as income or expenses.

The candidate model is signed, dated allocations associated with an operation, jar and account; transaction-backed entries also identify the relevant transaction/account leg. Initial allocations and same-account reallocations divide existing account money and create no bank movement. Unallocated funds reconcile account actuals minus signed jar balances. This is a technical design proposal, not yet deployed schema or a new product rule.

Implementation must cover:

1. Dated account openings (the current `Account` has no opening date), jar designation and ownership/currency validation, and persistent operation identity.
2. Initial allocation, transaction funding/withdrawal splits and same-account reallocation; outgoing funds identify the jar/unallocated sources.
3. Completed cross-account transfers with explicit user confirmation and idempotent pairing of imported statement sides.
4. One shared dated balance service used by JarBalances, BudgetActuals savings availability, CreateJar responses and account reconciliation. Planned amounts must never enter actual sums.
5. All write paths: create/update/delete transaction, transfers, both imports, allocation commits and account-opening edits. A protected manual-transfer path alone leaves bypasses.
6. Automatic savings-line jars in AddBudgetItem, templates and registration seeding must obtain a savings-account selection. Do not create valid-looking unbacked jars or assign an arbitrary account.
7. Account deletion must not detach jar/transaction history. Additive schema changes must preserve development data until handling is agreed; no invented opening dates or planned-as-actual backfill.
8. SQL transaction/concurrency protection for withdrawals. Read-then-save sufficiency checks alone can overdraw savings under concurrent requests.

Questions still relevant before affected foundation paths are activated: handling existing local sample data with missing account/opening date (O12), import reconciliation/order (O1), changing a jar's designated account after money/history exists (O3). Account/budget currency policy was answered in rule63; do not ask it again. Questions on unrelated features do not block the bounded fixes above.

## W03 Recurring lifecycle (rules 19–24, 57, 83; REVIEW concern 3) — 27 September 2026

**Scope.** A Recurring jar now has a persisted **open cycle** and a history of closed ones, and a missed deadline is a state the user must act on, never a silent roll. When a cycle's deadline arrives with the jar holding its target (measured at the end of the day before, rule 82), the cycle closes as **Funded** and the jar advances to the next deadline, its balance untouched (rules 19, 21). When it is short, the jar enters **Deadline missed** with the shortfall in view, plans the whole shortfall now and stays there (rule 20); reaching the target afterwards reads **Funded late** and the notice stays (rule 57). The user ends a cycle by **marking it paid** and choosing the payment that settled the bill — money out from the jar's account, or any expense on request; the app never infers which payment belongs to which cycle (rule 83) — or by **dismissing** the missed notice, which closes the cycle as missed (or funded late) and saves toward the next deadline without recording a payment or manufacturing money (rule 20). A payment before the deadline that empties the jar counts as met on time once marked paid (rule 22); only the actual remainder carries into the next cycle (rule 23); the target never changes on its own (rule 24). A cycle that rolled forward funded but whose payment was not recorded can still be given one from the card ("Paid it? Record the payment"). Money never moves on any of this. Goals are untouched.

## Review fixes after W04 (rules 21, 39, 51, 74, 79–80) — 28 September 2026

**Scope.** Five findings from an external review of PRs #260–#273, each verified against `main` at `13be67e` before it was changed, each fixed with a regression test that fails on that head. No schema change, no client change, no new endpoint.

- **Changing an empty jar's account rewrote the old account's history (rule 80; PR #261).** `JarLedgerReader.Derive` attributes a jar-line payment to the jar only when the jar's *current* account is the one the payment left, so once the jar named another account its past payments fell to unallocated in the old account while the allocations that funded them stayed: "jar 100, unallocated −100" and a backing deficit though no money moved. `UpdateJar` now calls the new `JarMovements.KeepLineAttributionAsync` before re-designating: every payment the jar's lines drew from the old account that has no stored answer is written down there as a stored operation, exactly as the reader derived it (`JarLedgerReader.Derive`/`LineJarsAsync` are shared with the writer). The old account then reads as before; nothing is stored on the new one. *Added on 28 September from Christopher's review of the PR:* editing that payment afterwards (payee, date, amount, a re-assignment) re-answers the jar prompt through `JarMovements.ApplyOutgoingAsync`, which looked the line's jar up in its current account and so replaced the kept operation with nothing — the old account read "jar 100, unallocated −100" again. `ApplyOutgoingAsync` now treats a line whose jar has since left the account as still drawing from that jar when the leg's stored operation says it did, and writes the attribution down again (a new amount is drawn from the jar too: 80 → jar 20, unallocated 0).
- **Editing a transfer into an expense could leave its destination negative (rule 74; PR #264).** The rule 74 guard checked the old and new source accounts only; the former destination lost its receipt unchecked. The handler now loads the transfer's destination, includes it in the accounts the guard checks, and drops the receipt leg's stored jar split with the receipt.
- **A source bank reference could hide the first receipt import (rule 51; PR #260).** `StatementReferences.KnownAsync` lent every inbound transfer's source reference to the destination account, so when both banks print the same end-to-end reference the destination's first receipt row was skipped as already imported and the transfer stayed in transit. The fallback now applies only to a transfer whose receipt is recorded (`ReceivedOn` set) — the legacy rows the `AccountOpeningDatesAndTransferLegs` migration marked received on their date — never to a transfer still in transit.
- **Editing a refunded purchase compared amounts in different currencies (rule 39, O5; PR #270).** `UpdateTransaction` summed the refunds' recorded amounts and compared them with the edited amount; `RefundRules` (create/link) compared base-currency values. ## W13 Joint accounts and privacy (rules 30–34; HANDOVER O8, P52–P60) — 29 September 2026

**Scope.** Accounts gain **holders** and per-login **privacy** (rules 30–34).
