### W01 foundation notes for the next developer

`Transaction` records account movement; `TransactionSplit` records budget attribution. Transfers currently cannot be split across budget lines. Add a distinct jar-allocation model rather than allowing transfers to masquerade as income or expenses.

The candidate model is signed, dated allocations associated with an operation, jar and account; transaction-backed entries also identify the relevant transaction/account leg. Initial allocations and same-account reallocations divide existing account money and create no bank movement. Unallocated funds reconcile account actuals minus signed jar balances. This is a technical design proposal, not yet deployed schema or a new product rule.

Implementation must cover:

1. Dated account openings (the current `Account` has no opening date), jar designation and ownership/currency validation, and persistent operation identity.
2. Initial allocation, transaction funding/withdrawal splits and same-account reallocation; outgoing funds identify the jar/unallocated sources.
3. Completed cross-account transfers with explicit user confirmation and idempotent pairing of imported statement sides.
4. One shared dated balance service used by JarBalances, BudgetActuals savings availability, CreateJar responses and account reconciliation. Planned amounts must never enter actual sums.
5. All write paths: create/update/delete transaction, transfers, both imports, allocation commits and account-opening edits. A protected manual-transfer path alone leaves bypasses.
6. Automatic savings-line jars in AddBudgetItem, templates and registration seeding must obtain a savings-account selection. Do not create valid-looking unbacked jars or assign an arbitrary account.
7. Account deletion must not detach jar/transaction history. Additive schema changes must preserve development data until handling is agreed; no invented opening dates or planned-as-actual backfill.
8. SQL transaction/concurrency protection for withdrawals. Read-then-save sufficiency checks alone can overdraw savings under concurrent requests.

Questions still relevant before affected foundation paths are activated: handling existing local sample data with missing account/opening date (O12), import reconciliation/order (O1), changing a jar's designated account after money/history exists (O3). Account/budget currency policy was answered in rule63; do not ask it again. Questions on unrelated features do not block the bounded fixes above.

## W03 Recurring lifecycle (rules 19–24, 57, 83; REVIEW concern 3) — 27 September 2026

**Scope.** A Recurring jar now has a persisted **open cycle** and a history of closed ones, and a missed deadline is a state the user must act on, never a silent roll. When a cycle's deadline arrives with the jar holding its target (measured at the end of the day before, rule 82), the cycle closes as **Funded** and the jar advances to the next deadline, its balance untouched (rules 19, 21). When it is short, the jar enters **Deadline missed** with the shortfall in view, plans the whole shortfall now and stays there (rule 20); reaching the target afterwards reads **Funded late** and the notice stays (rule 57). The user ends a cycle by **marking it paid** and choosing the payment that settled the bill — money out from the jar's account, or any expense on request; the app never infers which payment belongs to which cycle (rule 83) — or by **dismissing** the missed notice, which closes the cycle as missed (or funded late) and saves toward the next deadline without recording a payment or manufacturing money (rule 20). A payment before the deadline that empties the jar counts as met on time once marked paid (rule 22); only the actual remainder carries into the next cycle (rule 23); the target never changes on its own (rule 24). A cycle that rolled forward funded but whose payment was not recorded can still be given one from the card ("Paid it? Record the payment"). Money never moves on any of this. Goals are untouched.

## Review fixes after W04 (rules 21, 39, 51, 74, 79–80) — 28 September 2026

**Scope.** Five findings from an external review of PRs #260–#273, each verified against `main` at `13be67e` before it was changed, each fixed with a regression test that fails on that head. No schema change, no client change, no new endpoint.

- **Changing an empty jar's account rewrote the old account's history (rule 80; PR #261).** `JarLedgerReader.Derive` attributes a jar-line payment to the jar only when the jar's *current* account is the one the payment left, so once the jar named another account its past payments fell to unallocated in the old account while the allocations that funded them stayed: "jar 100, unallocated −100" and a backing deficit though no money moved. `UpdateJar` now calls the new `JarMovements.KeepLineAttributionAsync` before re-designating: every payment the jar's lines drew from the old account that has no stored answer is written down there as a stored operation, exactly as the reader derived it (`JarLedgerReader.Derive`/`LineJarsAsync` are shared with the writer). The old account then reads as before; nothing is stored on the new one. *Added on 28 September from Christopher's review of the PR:* editing that payment afterwards (payee, date, amount, a re-assignment) re-answers the jar prompt through `JarMovements.ApplyOutgoingAsync`, which looked the line's jar up in its current account and so replaced the kept operation with nothing — the old account read "jar 100, unallocated −100" again. `ApplyOutgoingAsync` now treats a line whose jar has since left the account as still drawing from that jar when the leg's stored operation says it did, and writes the attribution down again (a new amount is drawn from the jar too: 80 → jar 20, unallocated 0).
- **Editing a transfer into an expense could leave its destination negative (rule 74; PR #264).** The rule 74 guard checked the old and new source accounts only; the former destination lost its receipt unchecked. The handler now loads the transfer's destination, includes it in the accounts the guard checks, and drops the receipt leg's stored jar split with the receipt.
- **A source bank reference could hide the first receipt import (rule 51; PR #260).** `StatementReferences.KnownAsync` lent every inbound transfer's source reference to the destination account, so when both banks print the same end-to-end reference the destination's first receipt row was skipped as already imported and the transfer stayed in transit. The fallback now applies only to a transfer whose receipt is recorded (`ReceivedOn` set) — the legacy rows the `AccountOpeningDatesAndTransferLegs` migration marked received on their date — never to a transfer still in transit.
- **Editing a refunded purchase compared amounts in different currencies (rule 39, O5; PR #270).** `UpdateTransaction` summed the refunds' recorded amounts and compared them with the edited amount; `RefundRules` (create/link) compared base-currency values. ## W13 Joint accounts and privacy (rules 30–34; HANDOVER O8, P52–P60) — 29 September 2026

**Scope.** Accounts gain **holders** and per-login **privacy** (rules 30–34). An account lists its holders from People: none is the household's own account, one a personal account, two or more a joint account with one balance and one history (rule 30). A personal account whose holder has a login can be private to that login: the account, its balance and the transactions only it can see are not there for any other login — the owner included — and no one else can change them (rule 31). An account the holder adds for themselves starts private; one someone else adds for them starts shared and flags their sharing review; a login newly linked to a person who holds accounts gets a one-time **Review sharing** prompt that changes nothing until answered (rule 32). An account a jar uses — archived jars and jar allocation history included — can't be private, and choosing a private account for a jar shows the rule 34 warning with **Share the account and continue** or **Pick another account** (rules 33/34). Private money counts in household figures only on a budget line, where the others see it as "Private account", read-only; a private↔shared transfer shows on the shared side (P56/P57). O8's open questions were answered by the proposals P52–P59, posted to Christopher in the W13 thread before the build, and recorded in HANDOVER with P60 (deleting a private account), all applied unless he objects.

**Schema.** Migration `JointAccountsAndPrivacy`: table `AccountHolders` (AccountId, MemberId; composite key, cascade from the account and from the person, index on MemberId), table `AccountPrivacy` (AccountId key, cascade from the account; `UserId` the login it is private to; `SinceUtc`), and the bit `HouseholdMemberships.SharingReviewPending`. Nothing is backfilled: every existing account starts as the household's own, shared, and no review is pending.

**Enforcement, once for every handler (`ApplicationDbContext`).** The context takes `ICurrentUser` and reads as its login (`ViewerId`; null — background work, design time, tests that build it bare — filters nothing). Two global query filters: an `Account` private to another login is not there; a `Transaction` is there when any account it moves money in is visible, when it has no account, or when it (or one of its slices) is on a budget line. The filters read the unfiltered `AccountPrivacy` table, so the transaction filter does not inherit the account filter through navigation, and a hidden account's navigation reads null (the DTO then names it "Private account"). The write side is `SaveChanges`: any added, changed or deleted `Account`, `AccountHolder`, `Transaction`, `TransactionSplit` (through its transaction), `JarAllocation` or `Jar` that touches, before or after the change, an account private to another login is refused with 403 "This belongs to a private account. Only the login it is private to can change it."; the only exception is the visible side's bank leg of a private↔shared transfer (source: `DepartedOn`, `BankReference`, `Date`; receipt: `ReceivedOn`, `ReceiptBankReference`). A privacy row can be added, changed or removed only by the login it names.

WITH Legacy AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT j.Id AS JarId, j.Name AS Jar, a.AccountId
    FROM JarAllocationPostings p
    JOIN JarAllocations a ON a.Id = p.JarAllocationId
    JOIN Jars j ON j.Id = p.JarId
    WHERE a.AccountId <> j.FundingAccountId
      AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM JarAllocationPostings dp JOIN JarAllocations d ON d.Id = dp.JarAllocationId
                      WHERE d.Kind = 3 AND d.AccountId = a.AccountId AND dp.JarId = j.Id))
SELECT l.Jar, acc.Name AS OldAccount, acc.OpeningDate, t.Date, t.Amount, t.Payee,
       CASE WHEN acc.OpeningDate IS NULL THEN 'not written: no opening date'
            WHEN t.Date < acc.OpeningDate THEN 'not written: before the opening date'
            WHEN t.Date > '2026-09-29' THEN 'not written: after 29 Sep 2026'

JOIN Accounts acc ON acc.Id = l.AccountId
JOIN Transactions t ON t.AccountId = l.AccountId AND t.Type = 0

## Jar moves: accept and settle (R2-D1; R2-P3-8, R3-P3-4, K3; HANDOVER P51) — 29 September 2026

**Decision.** On 29 September 2026 at 14:05 UTC Christopher chose "Accept and settle" (R2-D1, the review of PR #276): a payment the jar's old account really made before the move, entered after it, goes on the jar's line and the move re-settles like a P51 correction. His other answer the same minute stays as #276 built it: a payment on the jar's line from the new account dated before the move is refused.

**What changed.**
- **Where the jar was held (`JarStretches`, new).** The jar's departures, from every account, are the record of its moves: a departure closes the stretch the jar spent in that account, and the stretch began on the jar's previous departure from any account (the move day belongs to both accounts), or has no recorded start when there is none. `LeftOn(jar, account, date)` is the covering departure's day when a recorded stretch held the jar there on that date; `HeldIn(jar, date)` names the account that did, when that stretch's start is recorded too (review P3-1, below). One query loads a jar's departures, and none is made when every jar line of a write is held by the paying account on its date.
- **The check (`TransactionInvariants.EnsureJarAccount`).** A payment on a jar's line is accepted when the paying account holds the jar on that date (`JarLedgerReader.HeldOn`, unchanged), or when a recorded stretch held it there (new). This replaces the kept-draw exemption (`KeptDrawJarsAsync`, removed): a stored draw of an earlier stretch is covered by that stretch, and so is a new entry, an entry moved there from another account, an existing one put back on the jar's line, and a re-split or re-date within the stretch, across a month too. Since the stretch is checked before the account, R3-P3-4 (a one-way move's kept payment could not be re-split keeping its jar slice, or re-dated across a month) and K3 (a kept payment taken off the jar's line could not be put back) are fixed. A refusal names the account that held the jar on the payment's date when a stretch says so ("'Holiday' is the jar Holiday, which was held in A on 10 Aug 2026. Record this payment from A so it comes out of the jar, or put it on another line."); otherwise the #276 messages stand. The whole-line check, the slice check, the date-edit check (`EnsureJarLinesArrivedAsync`) and the import's batch check all read the stretches.
- **Storing it (`JarMovements.ApplyOutgoingAsync`).** A line whose jar a recorded stretch held in the paying account on the payment's date is kept on the jar there, as a stored draw from an earlier stretch already was, and `ReplaceAsync`'s `SettleAsync` shifts the covering departure, so the old account holds nothing of the jar from the day it left, and the difference leaves its unallocated money that day. `CreateTransaction` now calls it for every expense on a line (it stores nothing when the reader derives the same, as before), and `CommitImport` for every row on a jar line its account did not hold on the row's date, since the reader can't derive a draw on a jar held elsewhere. Deleting the payment, or moving it off the line or to another account, clears the draw and settles the departure back (unchanged code).
- **The old account's ledger must read it.** With no opening date the payment is refused ("A has no opening date yet. Set A's opening date first, or record this payment on another line."): stored, it would count once a date is set, but not if the date set is later than the payment, while its departure's share would (R2-P1-1's phantom). Dated before the opening date, it is accepted and nothing is stored, as the ledger counts no entry before an account opened.
- **(R2-P3-8) The jar's payments list** (`GetJarPayments`) lists a payment as from the jar when its stored answer draws from the jar in whichever account paid it, so the old account's kept draws, the late ones included, read as from the jar there. A payment from the jar's own account with no stored answer is read as before.

**Known limits.**
- A move records a departure only when the old account's ledger carries the jar (`JarDepartures.RecordAsync`, unchanged): a jar that left an account with nothing in it (no money, no payments on its lines) has no stretch there, so a late payment from that account stays refused ("can't be added to its line from any account"). Recording a departure on every move would have fixed that for future moves, but an account with any jar operation can then neither be deleted nor made private (rule 33), which would block removing an account a jar was created in by mistake.
- **A stretch's start can be too early, and in that corner Christopher's "Refuse" for the earlier move does not hold.** A stretch is taken to begin on the jar's previous recorded departure. Two cases make that earlier than the day the jar really arrived: the jar passed through an account it carried nothing in (no departure is recorded there, as above), or it was moved before #275 and the startup backfill dated its departure on the old account's last entry (review P3-4, below). A payment on the jar's line from the later account, dated in the gap before the jar arrived there, is then accepted and kept on the jar in that account. Example: Holiday is in A with nothing in it, moves to B on 10 Aug, then leaves B for C today; a payment from B dated 5 Aug is accepted, and Holiday reads −50 in B on 5 Aug. The refusal no longer sends the user there (P3-1), and a first recorded stretch, whose start is never known, is not named. A durable fix stores, on each departure, the day the jar arrived in that account (its `FundingAccountSince` at the move). That needs a new column, so it is left for the backend final review.
- **The auto-categoriser (P2-E, still open) differs by path.** A manual entry (`CreateTransaction`) runs it before `ApplyOutgoingAsync`, so a payment it puts on the jar's line from the old account is kept on the jar at once and the move re-settles. A statement imported in one step (`ImportStatement`, which categorises the rows after creating them) still leaves such a payment on the old account's unallocated money until it is next edited, when `ApplyOutgoingAsync` keeps it on the jar if a stretch covers its date.
- R2-P3-10 is unchanged: the add form still shows the "paid from" picker on a payment from the old account on the jar's line (the client knows only where the jar is now). Leaving it on "Unallocated" is right; naming a jar is refused ("This payment is already on a jar's line; it draws from that jar.").
