# Agreed jar and account rules

These decisions were confirmed in conversation after the calculation review. They capture intended behaviour; current implementation status is in [DEVELOPMENT-LOG.md](DEVELOPMENT-LOG.md). Unresolved details must be discussed with the user rather than inferred. This repository copy is the maintained source of truth. Start with [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md) for scope, priorities and unresolved decisions. Later explicit corrections supersede earlier statements; cross-references below identify the main cases.

## Confirmed

1. Planned contributions do not increase actual jar savings. Actual contributions must be supported by recorded transactions, either a whole transaction or an allocated portion of a split transaction.
2. Every jar must have one explicitly designated savings account. Multiple jars may share an account, but a jar's funds reside in its one designated account. Money elsewhere cannot count toward that jar's saved balance. This supersedes the earlier allowance for counting money earmarked in a current account.
3. Incoming money may be allocated across jars associated with the receiving savings account. Any remainder stays unallocated in that account.
4. Every outgoing movement from a shared savings account must identify the jar balances and/or unallocated funds it draws from. Splits are supported.
5. A jar may become negative by default when more is withdrawn than it holds. Users may optionally reallocate to cover the shortfall during the action. Merely changing a planned amount must not create actual savings.
6. For jars in the same account, reallocation decreases the source jar and increases the destination jar without changing the account balance.
7. For jars in different accounts, explain that a bank transfer is required. The user's confirmation is explicitly **“I've completed this transfer.”** Record the completed transfer and update both affected account balances and both affected jar balances exactly once. Do not silently treat it as only a jar reallocation. Rules 69/71 refine the timing: record confirmed departure, keep money in transit until receipt, and apply each bank leg only on its own posting date.
8. Importing a bank statement must not count an already recorded transfer again. Suggest a match to the existing transfer and ask for user confirmation, as agreed in the conversation. Matching the statement entry must not apply another balance movement. Matching criteria and handling ambiguous candidates remain to be specified.
9. At initial setup, users may allocate money already held in a savings account between its jars, leaving the remainder unallocated. This divides the existing account balance; it does not record a new deposit or increase the account balance.
10. A savings account must never have a negative recorded balance. Individual jars may go negative, but outgoing spending/transfers must not exceed the source savings account's available recorded funds. Reallocating between jars in that account cannot remedy an account-level shortage because it does not add account funds.
11. An imported entry that would make a savings account negative must be clearly highlighted and held for review. Require reconciliation of the discrepancy before applying it; do not silently post it or invent a balancing deposit. Batch ordering and handling missing history remain implementation details to resolve.
12. Confirmed schedule example: a jar created in September with nothing saved and a 300-unit target due on 1 December plans 100 units each in September, October and November. The full amount must be ready before December begins; December is not a contribution month in this example. Mid-month deadlines are not yet decided.
13. At month rollover, missed contributions cause the remaining requirement to be recalculated from actual savings over the remaining contribution months, with the increased monthly requirement clearly highlighted. Confirmed example: only 50 units actually saved by the end of September against the 300-unit target due 1 December leaves 250 units to save, requiring 125 in October and 125 in November.
14. Within a month, show the month's plan, actual contributions that month, and the remaining contribution separately. Confirmed example: October's plan of 125 stays at 125 after a contribution of 50; contributed this month is 50 and still to contribute is 75. Do not reduce the displayed plan merely because a contribution has been recorded.
15. The target/deadline belongs to the individual jar, not its designated savings account. The user qualified the confirmed deadline-based schedule/recalculation rules above as applying to jars with a target amount and deadline. No-deadline modes are specified below.
16. An ongoing savings jar without a target amount or deadline uses a fixed monthly planned contribution chosen by the user. Missing part of that plan does not automatically increase later months' plans. Actual saved balance still comes only from transactions allocated to that jar and held in its designated savings account.
17. For a jar with a target amount, the user must supply either a fixed monthly contribution or a deadline date. A deadline lets the app calculate the required monthly contribution; a fixed monthly contribution allows saving toward the target without a deadline. No automatic twelve-month default is implied. Estimated completion display still needs confirmation.
18. For a one-off target using a fixed monthly contribution, cap the final planned contribution at the amount remaining to reach the target and stop further planned contributions once the target is actually reached. Confirmed example: a 250-unit target at 100 per month plans 100, 100, then 50. Planned contributions alone do not establish that the target has been reached; actual savings rules continue to apply.
19. For recurring jars, actual leftover savings carry into the next cycle and reduce its funding requirement. Confirmed example: an annual insurance jar with a 300-unit target and 50 left after this year's bill needs another 250 for the next cycle. Beginning a cycle must not reset or duplicate the actual balance. Successful-cycle rollover and missed deadlines are covered below.
20. A recurring jar that misses its funding deadline must enter a persistent **Deadline missed** state rather than silently advancing the date. Confirmed example: only 450 saved against a 600 target due 1 December leaves a highlighted 150 shortfall after the deadline. The state remains until explicitly dismissed by the user. Dismissal resumes saving/planning toward the next deadline, preserves the actual jar/account balances, and does not record an expense as paid or manufacture the missing funds. How subsequent contributions interact with an undismissed missed-deadline state remains to be specified.
21. If a recurring jar reaches its target on time, it automatically advances to the next scheduled deadline once the current deadline passes. Rollover leaves jar and account balances unchanged. The user must record the actual payment transaction before those balances decrease; rollover itself must not create a payment or consume funds.
22. A recurring cycle that was fully funded and then paid before its deadline counts as successfully completed even if the payment leaves the jar empty. Confirmed example: save 600, pay the insurance on 28 November, and reach the 1 December deadline with a zero balance. Do not incorrectly enter Deadline missed. Completion must account for the funding/payment history for that cycle, not just the balance on the deadline. How to identify a cycle-fulfilling payment still needs agreement; lower actual bills are covered below.
23. If the actual bill is below the planned target, the user may mark the cycle complete after saving and paying the actual bill. Only the actual remaining jar balance carries into the next cycle: 600 saved and 550 paid leaves 50 to carry forward; 550 saved and 550 paid leaves zero. An unused part of a planned target is not money and must never be credited to the jar.
24. The next recurring cycle retains the existing target unless the user explicitly changes it. A lower actual bill does not automatically lower that target. Confirmed example: a 600-unit target remains 600 after a 550-unit bill; if 50 of actual savings remains, the next cycle needs another 550, while a zero carry-forward leaves the full 600 to save.
25. Permanent deletion is available only for unused jars. Jars with transaction history must instead be archived, preserving their past activity. The precise unused-jar eligibility checks (including initial allocations and existing planned lines) still need to be specified.
26. A jar must have an actual balance of zero before archiving. Reallocate any positive balance to another jar or the account's unallocated funds, or cover a negative balance, using the agreed reallocation/transfer rules. Archiving must not discard money, erase a shortfall, or invent an adjustment to force the balance to zero.
27. Archiving cancels the jar's remaining planned contribution for the current month and all future planned contributions, including those in budgets already created. Preserve actual transactions and historical activity; cancelling a plan must not change actual jar/account balances.
28. People and Allocation functionality must use income entered in the selected month's Budget. Saved per-person income defaults are only helpers for populating a new budget; they must not supply a separate or fallback income pool for that month's calculations.
29. Retain household cost splitting for users who need it, as an optional feature explicitly enabled for their household. The user's household does not personally need the calculation, but the product must serve other households too. Use the same selected month and figures as Budget; support equal or income-based suggested contributions. Suggestions remain plans, and actual transfers require the explicit completed-transfer confirmation already agreed. The placement or removal of the separate Allocation page, and the fate of existing pocket-money/surplus-distribution controls, have not been decided.
30. Support joint bank accounts alongside personal accounts. A joint account is one account linked to multiple household members, with a single shared balance and transaction history. Its balance and transactions must not be duplicated for each owner. The user confirmed this matches their bank's joint-account arrangement. Editing permissions, individual ownership shares, joint-account visibility to non-owners and effects on optional cost-splitting calculations remain to be discussed.
31. Personal-account owners choose whether to share their account information with the household or keep it private. Do not impose one visibility policy on all users. Privacy controls are available only when the person has an actual linked user/login. New personal accounts for those users start private until their owner explicitly shares them. For people listed in People without a login, their accounts remain visible within the household under its existing access permissions. Sharing scope, treatment of linked jars and transactions, and inclusion in shared totals remain to be specified. Privacy belongs to the login that chose it: an account passes only to a login with the same verified email (Christopher, 29 September 2026, "Same email only"); one whose login has left the household stays hidden from everyone, and the budget owner may erase it unseen behind its typed name, removing only what nobody else could see: anything the household already saw (an entry on a budget line, a refund of a shared purchase, a shared account's side of a transfer with it, with its bank reference and fee) keeps counting without the account. Decided by Christopher on 30 September 2026 ("Add", then "Keep shared").
32. When a person later obtains a linked login, prompt them to review sharing settings for their existing accounts. Existing visibility remains unchanged until they choose; do not automatically make previously shared accounts private merely because the login was linked.
33. **Corrected by the user:** privacy is available only for entirely personal, unshared jars/accounts. An account cannot be private while it is used for a shared jar. Shared jars and the accounts backing them must remain fully visible; do not implement a shared jar over a hidden account or partial-account disclosure as previously suggested. A user may choose whether to expose an eligible personal account, but cannot hide something used for shared purposes. The audience meant by shared visibility and treatment of personal jars within a shared account still need precise definition.
34. When sharing a jar would conflict with its backing account's privacy, present a clear warning explaining what would become visible and let the user decide how to proceed. Do not automatically expose the account or move funds. The resolution must respect the agreed rule that an account backing a shared jar cannot remain private; any funds movement must follow the existing completed-transfer confirmation and balance rules. Exact warning actions and wording remain UI details to define.
35. For a deadline-based contribution schedule, put the rounding remainder into the final contribution so the planned contributions sum exactly to the remaining target. Confirmed example: 100 units over three months plans 33.33, 33.33, then 33.34. The projected completion date must reflect that schedule instead of adding another month for the final cent. Recalculation from actual savings must preserve the same exact-total property.
36. For a deadline partway through a month, default to completing planned funding by the end of the previous month. Offer an explicit option to include a contribution in the deadline month, explaining that the money must reach the designated savings account before the deadline. Confirmed example: 300 needed by 15 December, starting in September, defaults to 100 in September/October/November; if the user includes an eligible December contribution, plan 75 in September/October/November/December. Keep the actual deadline at 15 December and distinguish it from the default planned funding completion date of 30 November. Do not mark the deadline missed merely because the earlier planned completion date passes.
37. Spending reports must include recorded expenses even when they have not been assigned to a budget category. Show the unassigned amount separately as **Uncategorised** and flag it for review. Confirmed example: 500 actually spent, of which 400 is categorised, must report 500 total with 100 uncategorised. Categorising an existing expense later moves it between reporting groups without increasing the total. This does not resolve the separate questions of reporting periods, pending FX, income completeness or privacy scope.
38. Link a refund to the original expense and reduce net spending in that expense's category, while retaining both transactions in history. Do not classify the refund as new income. Confirmed example: a 100-unit grocery purchase followed by a 20-unit refund yields 80 net grocery spending when considering both transactions. Cross-month attribution is specified below.
39. Keep purchases and refunds on their actual transaction dates. A refund reduces spending in the month received, remains linked to the original purchase, and does not rewrite earlier months. Confirmed example: a September grocery purchase of 100 and an October refund of 20 show September spending of 100, October net spending of -20 if there are no other grocery purchases, and combined net spending of 80. Clearly label the refund so negative net spending is understandable. The account balance changes on the refund's actual date.

40. An opening balance represents the balance at the start of its chosen date. Earlier transactions may remain available as history but must not be added to that opening balance again. Only transactions dated on or after the opening date change the balance forward from that opening amount. Confirmed example: an opening balance of 1,000 on 1 September already includes an August deposit of 200; importing that deposit later must not increase the balance to 1,200. Migration of existing opening balances and historical reporting before the cutoff remain unresolved.

41. Reports must consistently distinguish planned/projected income from actual income received and show their difference. Actual income includes recorded income even when uncategorised, excluding transfers between the user's accounts and refunds. The difference can be positive or negative because income naturally varies; do not label it a shortfall or imply that variation is an error. The confirmed display label is **Variance**, calculated as actual income minus projected income. For a projection of 3,000, actual income of 2,800 gives variance -200 and actual income of 3,200 gives variance +200.

42. The app must not automatically apply or assume currency conversion in recorded amounts. The user's initial instruction to remove all conversion code is qualified by rule 44: conversions may be calculated as suggestions requiring explicit user approval. Remove or change paths that apply conversion without approval, including background rate reconciliation and automatic exchange-rate multiplication in balances/reports. Do not substitute an implicit 1:1 rate. The previously proposed pending-conversion reporting workflow was not approved. A review/migration workflow for previously converted live records is not required, per the user's pre-launch clarification in rule 45; no application code has been changed yet.

43. Bank-provided or user-entered monetary amounts are authoritative. Do not apply an external rate to replace an already supplied amount or derive a fee from a difference between amounts. Rule 44 allows a suggested conversion only when the required amount is missing; it does not authorise assumed fees.

44. Each budget uses one currency. For a foreign-currency purchase, use the bank-provided or user-entered amount in the budget currency. When that amount is missing, the app may suggest a converted amount where applicable, but the user must explicitly approve it before it is applied to recorded amounts, balances or budget/report totals. An unapproved suggestion is not actual money. Already supplied amounts do not require this conversion-approval step and must not be replaced by a suggestion. Rate source, suggestion presentation and treatment of records awaiting approval remain to be specified.

45. The user clarified that the app is not live yet and there should not be existing live transactions converted by the app. Do not build the proposed historical-conversion review workflow on the assumption that production records need remediation. This clarification does not establish that development/test data is absent and does not authorise deleting it.

46. Changing a jar's target or deadline must show the revised contribution schedule for user confirmation, then update the current and future planned contributions, including plans in budgets already created. Preserve past months and actual transactions, jar balances and account balances. This explicit, confirmed edit is distinct from ordinary within-month contributions, which do not themselves change the displayed monthly plan under rule 14.

47. Lowering a jar target below its actual saved balance preserves the entire actual balance and shows the excess above target. Confirmed example: a jar holding 900 whose target is reduced to 800 still holds 900 and shows 100 above target. Stop further planned contributions while the target is funded; any movement of the excess requires an explicit user reallocation under the existing rules. The target edit itself does not move or remove money. Existing recurring-cycle rules continue to apply.

48. Past budgets show jar balances as of the end of the selected month, excluding subsequent activity. Confirmed example: a jar holding 300 at September month-end and receiving 100 in October shows 300 in September's budget while its current balance is 400. Historical views must not use today's/all-time balance. Later-entered, backdated transactions are covered by rule 49.

49. Adding a genuinely missing transaction or correcting an erroneous transaction updates historical actual figures using the transaction's actual date, and carries the correction forward through affected balances. Confirmed example: importing a missing September deposit of 50 in October changes September's jar balance from 300 to 350. This is a correction of the recorded history, distinct from attributing an October movement to September; the opening-balance cutoff and duplicate-import rules still apply. This does not authorise rewriting past planned budgets.

50. When an imported statement entry has multiple plausible recorded transfer matches, show the candidates and require the user to select the correct match or identify the entry as a separate transaction. Do not resolve the ambiguity automatically or determine a match from the amount alone. Confirmed example: two recorded transfers of 100 could match one statement entry of 100. Matching an existing transfer must not apply its balance movement again, as required by rule 8.

51. Imported debit and credit entries representing the two sides of one transfer must link to one transfer, retaining both bank references associated with their respective accounts. Apply each account's balance movement once. Matching the second statement entry confirms the other side of the existing transfer; it must not create or apply another transfer. Confirmed example: transferring 100 from current to savings and importing both statements changes current by -100 and savings by +100, not -200/+200. The agreed user-confirmation and ambiguous-match rules continue to apply.

52. Rounding when splitting a non-negative amount between people must preserve the exact total and must never create a negative share. Distribute only the available currency units. Confirmed example: splitting EUR 0.02 equally among four people yields two shares of 0.01 and two shares of 0.00, never 0.01, 0.01, 0.01, -0.01. Selection of recipients for leftover cents has not been decided. This rule concerns rounding of allocations, not the separately permitted negative jar balances.

53. Link a person's pocket-money budget line to their permanent identifier, not their display name. Renaming the person updates the displayed name without creating another allocation or changing the total. Syncing after a rename must reuse the existing linked line rather than leaving the original amount and adding a duplicate. Archived people's planned allocations are covered by rule 54.

54. Archiving a person stops their pocket-money planned allocations from the next month onward, including future budgets already created. For the current month, ask the user whether to keep or cancel the remaining planned amount. Preserve historical transactions. Cancelling planned allocations must not alter actual balances or remove recorded payments.

55. One-off jars have an explicit user action to mark their purpose completed. A completed jar must not restart planned contributions merely because its savings were spent. Confirmed example: save 1,000 for a laptop, record the 1,000 purchase, and mark the jar completed; its zero balance must not trigger another 1,000 savings plan. Preserve the purchase and saving history. Completion itself does not record a payment or change actual balances. Positive remaining balances are covered by rule 56; negative-balance eligibility and reopening behaviour remain to be discussed.

56. A one-off jar may be marked completed while retaining a positive actual balance. Confirmed example: save 1,000, pay 950 for the laptop, and complete the jar with 50 remaining. Keep that 50 visible in the jar until the user explicitly reallocates it; completion must not move or discard it. Archiving still requires a zero balance under rule 26.

57. If a recurring jar reaches its target after an undismissed missed deadline, show **Funded late** and retain the missed-deadline notice until the user explicitly dismisses it. Confirmed example: 450 saved against a 600 target at the deadline, followed by another 150 a week later. Late funding must not rewrite the cycle as having met its deadline or silently advance it. This resolves rule 20's question about subsequent contributions that finish funding while the missed-deadline notice remains undismissed. The deposit changes actual balances normally; the status change itself does not move money or record payment.

58. For a target jar with a fixed monthly contribution and no deadline, show an estimated time to reach the target based on the remaining actual funding requirement and planned monthly contribution. Confirmed example: target 1,000, actual savings 400 and monthly contribution 100 means approximately six more monthly contributions, assuming those contributions occur and no withdrawals. Clearly present this as an estimate, not a deadline; passing the estimate must not trigger Deadline missed. This confirms the estimated-completion display left open in rule 17. A precise calendar completion date is not implied by this example.

59. If a required jar contribution exceeds the remaining budget income available for planning, show the full required contribution and clearly flag the resulting budget over-allocation. Confirmed example: a revised schedule requires 200 per month while only 150 remains in the budget; show the required 200 and the 50 over-allocation. The user decides which plans to adjust. Do not silently reduce the required contribution, change other plans or imply that the budget balances. Planned over-allocation does not create actual money or permit an otherwise invalid account withdrawal.

60. When removing a jar's savings line from a monthly budget, offer an explicit choice between skipping that month's planned contribution and archiving the jar to stop future contributions. Skipping a month leaves the jar active; archiving follows the previously agreed zero-balance requirement and cancellation rules. Neither choice deletes actual transactions or changes actual balances. Do not silently interpret removing a monthly plan as permanently stopping the jar, or recreate the intentionally skipped month's contribution through routine synchronisation. Preview of a deadline-based skip is covered by rule 61.

61. Before confirming a skipped contribution for a deadline-based jar, show the revised contribution schedule and explain its impact. Confirmed example: a 300 target due at the start of December with nothing saved initially requires 100 in September, October and November; skipping September shows revised contributions of 150 in October and November before the user confirms. Calculate the preview from actual savings and remaining eligible contribution months, preserving the existing deadline and exact-total rounding rules. The preview itself does not change plans or balances.

62. If a recurring deadline's intended day does not exist in a month, use that month's last valid day while retaining the intended day for subsequent occurrences. Confirmed example: a monthly deadline on the 31st falls on 28 or 29 February, then 31 March, then 30 April. Account for leap years, never produce invalid dates, and do not permanently shift the intended day because a shorter month required adjustment. The preview and saved schedule must apply the same rule.

## Required accounting checks

- For each savings account, the signed sum of its jar balances plus unallocated funds must reconcile to the recorded account balance. Negative jars must remain visible in this calculation, rather than being clamped to zero.
- A same-account reallocation conserves the combined jar/unallocated total and leaves account money unchanged.
- A cross-account transfer conserves the combined account balances plus money in transit. Once both bank legs have posted, a 50-unit transfer means source account −50, destination account +50, source jar −50 and destination jar +50. Between departure and arrival, the 50 is in transit and belongs to neither jar (rule 69). These are corresponding views of the same movement, not independent amounts of money.
- Transaction splits must sum to the transaction amount. Rounded allocations of a non-negative pool must also preserve its total without creating negative shares. A repeated import/match must not duplicate its effect.
- No unapproved currency conversion or inferred fees: use authoritative bank/user amounts, or explicitly approved conversion suggestions, as agreed above. Each budget has one currency. Account currency boundaries, representation of explicitly supplied cross-currency transfer amounts/fees, reversals and non-savings-account overdraft handling remain to be discussed.

63. For the first release, every account linked to a budget must use that budget's currency. A EUR budget and its jars are backed by EUR accounts; foreign purchases use the bank-provided or user-entered EUR charge, or a suggested EUR amount explicitly approved under rule 44. Different account currencies must not be silently treated as equivalent amounts.

64. For equal splits, distribute leftover cents in the displayed member order. For income-based or balance-based splits, assign leftover cents to the largest fractional remainders, resolving ties in displayed order. Confirmed examples: EUR 0.02 equally across four people gives the first two EUR 0.01 each; calculated shares 0.006 and 0.004 become 0.01 and 0.00. This resolves rule 52's recipient selection question. Preserve the exact total without negative rounded shares; it does not change the separate policy discussion about currency precision beyond cents.

65. Existing development data may be discarded: the user explicitly instructed, "Scrap all current data and we will start with a fresh database." This supersedes the earlier request to carry forward local/test ledgers and removes the need for a legacy-data setup/migration flow. Rebuild the project's verified development database for the new model; this does not authorise deleting source files or unrelated databases. No legacy opening dates, account links or actual savings need to be invented or backfilled. This authorisation is not a record that a reset has already occurred.

66. Deliver each separate development change through its own committed and pushed pull request, following the repository's PR history. The user authorised taking each change individually through to production and confirmed the application is not live. Validate each change and its deployment before promoting the next; preserve the existing deployment workflow. This is standing release authorisation for the agreed development work, not an instruction to bypass failing functional checks or reset unrelated databases.

67. For an as-of date before an account's opening-balance date, display the balance as unavailable ("Not available before opening date"). Do not display zero or reconstruct it from incomplete earlier transaction history. The opening amount takes effect at the start of its recorded date, as already agreed in rule 40.

68. Reallocating money must not make the source jar negative. Confirmed example: an account has 500, Holiday has 100 and Repairs has 400; moving 150 from Holiday to Repairs is not allowed. Holiday can supply at most its available 100, and any remainder needs an explicitly chosen other source. This differs from an actual payment, which may make a jar negative under rule 5. A reallocation across accounts also needs sufficient actual savings-account money and the previously agreed completed-transfer confirmation.

69. Retain each bank's posting date for a transfer. Confirmed example: money leaves one savings account on 30 September and arrives at the other on 1 October. Debit the source account and source jar on 30 September; credit the destination account and destination jar on 1 October. Between those dates, show the amount as **in transit**, counted in neither jar. Do not force both movements onto a single date or make the receiving jar available before receipt. This resolves O1's differing posting-date question, including month boundaries. Rule 71 confirms recording departure before the receiving date is known.

70. The user reiterated: "total value of jars allocated against a saving account cannot exceed the value of same account" and confirmed that signed reconciliation alone does not satisfy this limit. Positive jar balances must be backed by that account's actual money. Confirmed example: after a 150 Holiday payment from an account of 500 with Holiday 100 and Repairs 400, the account has 350, Holiday is −50 and Repairs is 400. Require an explicit 50 reallocation from Repairs to cover Holiday: the resulting balances are Holiday 0, Repairs 350 and account 350. Negative jars must not hide an excess of positive allocations over bank money. The app must not silently select a covering jar, change the recorded payment or create money. Read calculations may expose the deficit for correction; source availability alone must not authorise a completed reallocation without validating the resulting account backing. This refines rule 5: negative jar balances remain observable, but an uncovered account-backing deficit requires explicit correction.

71. The user approved the **departure → in transit → confirmed receipt** workflow after reviewing mainstream approaches. Once the user confirms the money has left Account A, record that departure and decrease A and its source jar on the supplied departure date. Leave receipt unknown and show the amount in transit until the user confirms receipt or confirms a match to the receiving statement. Only then credit Account B and its jar on the supplied receiving date. Do not invent an arrival date. If both legs have already been recorded, matching either statement must only attach/confirm its evidence, without another monetary effect. The receipt-first case with missing departure details remains a separate question; this answer does not resolve it.

72. Once the user identifies a received statement entry as a transfer from another owned account, retain it as **Pending transfer review** if its departure date is unknown. Preserve the receiving bank's amount and receipt date; do not post either transfer leg or make the receipt available to jars until the user supplies the departure date or confirms a match to the source statement. Highlight the pending receipt and the temporary discrepancy between the app's posted balance and the receiving statement. Confirmed example: A shows 500 and B shows 200, with a 100 receipt into B. Do not credit B to 300 while leaving A at 500. Once departure details are supplied, record A 400 and B 300 using each bank's actual date. Do not invent a departure date, identify the sending account from amount alone, or treat a pending receipt as income or money in transit. This resolves rule 71's receipt-first question; matching criteria and correction workflows remain separate.

73. **Every jar is created with a designated account.** No creation path — the Jars tab, a Budget-page Savings group line, a quick-start template or registration seeding — may create a jar without one. Creating a jar from a Savings group must present the same form and produce the same result as creating it from the Jars tab (account, target, deadline and the rest), not a name-only line with a hidden jar. Any account type may back a jar, with savings accounts encouraged; credit cards are excluded by rule 75. Registration seeding and the quick-start templates are under review following onboarding research; until that review concludes they must not create jars. (Resolves O3a, 26 September 2026.)

74. **No account may have a negative recorded balance**, whatever its type and even when the bank permits an overdraft. This extends rule 10 from savings accounts to every account. A manual entry that would breach it is refused with the balance and date stated; an imported statement is evaluated row by row in date order and only the breaching rows are held for review, with no "post anyway". A held row is resolved by recording the missing deposit or opening correction and re-applying it, by correcting its amount or account, or by discarding it. Rows dated before the account's opening date are history only (rule 40), never change the balance and are therefore never held.

75. **Credit cards are not accounts.** The credit-card account type is withdrawn from account creation on the fresh database; should any such account exist it follows rules 74 and 70 like every other account.

76. **No bank identifiers are kept** (changed 27 Oct 2026). AllotJar does not ask for or store an IBAN, BIC/SWIFT, sort code or account number on any account; the account's name is how the user tells accounts apart. (Before: they were optional, format-checked account details.)

77. **A jar's "saved so far" is an initial allocation, not an opening balance.** It divides money already in the designated account (rule 9): a dated allocation from the account's unallocated funds, defaulting to today, backdatable to no earlier than the account's opening date, and validated against the unallocated balance as of that date. The legacy jar opening-balance field is retired; on the fresh database nothing is backfilled (rule 65).

78. **Incoming money prompts for allocation when it is recorded.** When money reaches an account that backs at least one jar — manual income or deposit, a transfer receipt confirmed by any path (manual, statement match, allocation commit), or an imported credit in the import review table — the app asks how much goes to each of that account's jars, defaulting to unallocated. The prompt may be dismissed; unallocated money can be allocated later from the Jars page. Confirmed example: 500 arrives in Savings, which backs Holiday and Car; the user enters Holiday 300, Car 200; unallocated is unchanged.

79. **Outgoing money identifies its jar through the budget line.** A payment from a jar-backed account assigned to a jar's savings budget line (or split slices assigned to such lines) draws from that jar; a payment assigned to no line draws from unallocated; a payment assigned to a line that is not a jar prompts "which jar does this come from?", defaulting to unallocated. Confirmed examples from Savings 1,000 (Holiday 400, Car 300, unallocated 300): a 250 hotel bill on the Holiday line leaves Holiday 150 and the account 750; a 100 payment with no line leaves unallocated 200; a 500 repair on the Car line leaves Car −200 (rule 5) with positive jars 400 still backed by the account's 500 (rule 70).

80. **Changing a jar's designated account requires a zero balance.** The change is allowed only while the jar's actual balance is zero and no transfer touching it is in transit; otherwise the app explains what the jar holds and offers "Move money", the cross-account reallocation recorded as a confirmed bank transfer (rules 7, 68, 71). History stays with the previous account. A field change never infers a transfer. This follows the pot model of mainstream banking apps, where a pot belongs to one account.

81. **Onboarding is accounts-first with no sample data.** Registration no longer offers sample data and seeds nothing; the Budget page shows a first-run checklist until each step is done — add accounts with opening balances, create the month's budget (blank or from a template), add jars from an account. Quick-start templates keep their income and expense structure and no longer contain savings lines; jars are only ever created through the one jar form, which requires an account (rule 73). The startup repair seeder that retro-created "Emergency Fund" jars is removed. Agreed on 26 September 2026 after reviewing EveryDollar, YNAB, Monarch, Goodbudget, Actual Budget, Monzo Pots and Starling Spaces; this closes the registration/template review left open by rule 73.

82. **Jar schedules use plain calendar dates.** There is no household timezone: a deadline is a calendar date, the deadline day itself is never a contribution day (the money must be in the account before it), and the current month counts as a contribution month until it ends, whatever today's date — a jar created on 27 September with 300 due on 1 December still plans September, October and November. Balance cutoffs stay as rules 40, 48 and 67. Agreed on 27 September 2026 (HANDOVER O2, question 2 before W02).

83. **A recurring cycle is fulfilled by an explicit "mark cycle paid" that chooses the payment.** The user marks the cycle as paid and picks the payment transaction that fulfilled it; the app never infers which payment belongs to which cycle, early payments across cycles included. Agreed on 27 September 2026 (HANDOVER O3, for W03; rules 21–24 unchanged).

84. **Permanent deletion is only for a jar that never held money.** A jar may be deleted only when no money was ever allocated to it or spent from it; deletion removes its planned Savings lines. Every other jar is archived under rules 25–27, and archiving remains available for an unused jar too. Agreed on 27 September 2026 (HANDOVER O3, for W04).

85. **One preselected currency for the budget and its accounts.** The whole budget and the bank accounts linked to it use one currency chosen up front. A bank converts a foreign transaction itself, so the bank's amount is what is recorded; for an entry that is not a bank transaction the user enters the converted amount, or the app may suggest one that the user must confirm before it is applied. This restates rules 42–44 and 63 as the answer to the currency boundary questions. Agreed on 27 September 2026 (HANDOVER O5).

86. **StraightLine, DailyRate and ProportionalPool are retired.** A jar saves either by a deadline (`TargetByDate`, rules 12–15, 35–36) or at a fixed monthly amount (`FixedMonthly`, rules 16–18, 58); existing jars migrate to the deadline method and the cover-window columns are dropped. The importer tool that wrote the retired modes is dead code to be removed. Currency minor-unit policy beyond cents stays open. Agreed on 27 September 2026 (HANDOVER O6, question 1 before W02).

87. **Every recorded transfer is a match candidate, nearest date first.** A statement row is offered every recorded transfer whose leg on that account is still open to a statement, in the same currency, whatever its date: no date window. They are listed nearest date first and the user chooses (rules 8, 50). A transfer recorded in the app (by hand or by the income allocation) without a bank reference is a candidate for its statement, and confirming it takes the bank's date (rule 69) without moving money again. Agreed on 29 September 2026 (HANDOVER O1, answer 1 "Keep").

88. **A reused bank reference with different details is shown, not skipped.** A statement row whose bank reference the account already knows is skipped only when its date, amount and direction are also the same. Otherwise it is listed, flagged with the earlier entry's details and unticked, for the user to decide; imported, it is stored under a reference derived from its own details, so importing the statement again still changes nothing (rule 51). Agreed on 29 September 2026 (HANDOVER O1, answer 2 "Show").

89. **Transfers are edited in place.** A transfer's accounts, amount, dates and fee are corrected by editing it, not by deleting and re-recording it. A leg confirmed by a bank statement keeps the bank's account, date and amount. Agreed on 29 September 2026 (HANDOVER O1, answer 3 "Edit").

90. **A transfer can be marked returned.** When the money left the source and came back without arriving, the transfer is marked returned on the date it came back: the source has the money again from that date, the jars it left return to where they were, nothing stays in transit, and both movements stay in the source's history. A statement credit on the source can confirm the return. Agreed on 29 September 2026 (HANDOVER O1, answer 4 "Returned").

91. **A transfer fee is entered explicitly on a budget line.** The user enters a transfer's fee and picks the budget line it is spent from; the source is then debited the transfer plus the fee and the destination receives the transfer, so the transfer matches both statements. A difference between the two statements is never assumed to be a fee (rule 44): the import proposes it and the user confirms it with a line. Agreed on 29 September 2026 (HANDOVER O1, answer 5 "Fee"). The import proposes a fee only when it is at most 10% of the transfer, and a fee confirmed at import always names its line; larger differences are other payments, and a bank's fixed charge on a small transfer is entered by hand on the transfer. Decided by Christopher on 29 September 2026 ("10% or 10") and changed by him on 30 September 2026 to "10% only", which drops the flat 10 and with it the question of which currency the 10 is in.

92. **A month given without a day is on time until it ends.** A jar's date can be just a month (jar setup plan, PR 2). The money is still planned to be ready when the month begins (rule 12), but the month is not missed until it is over: while it runs, a jar still short reads **Behind** (a warning), and a recurring jar reads **Deadline missed** (rule 20) only from the 1st of the next month. A payment any day in that month is on time (rule 22), and a cycle short when the month began counts as funded on time from the first day the jar holds its target in that month, even if the bill is then paid from it. A full date is unchanged, and a goal is never "missed". Agreed on 7 October 2026 ("how about showing a warning while still in July and show missed in August?").

93. **Money leaving a recurring jar recalculates its plan.** Any payment or transfer that takes money out of a recurring jar, any move of its money to another jar, any edit or deletion of one, and taking back money that was put into it (an income's or transfer's jar share lowered or deleted), replans that jar's current and later months from what it actually holds, whether or not the payment is marked as the cycle's. Confirmed example: a 120 yearly jar due in September holds 120 when September comes, so the cycle rolls forward and plans 0; the bill paid from the jar in September and not marked paid leaves 0, so September and the months after it plan 10 (120 over the twelve months to next September). Marking a payment paid is still the user's choice and is never inferred (rule 83), so a bill paid before the deadline and not marked paid keeps the cycle open, plans to refill it by the deadline, and reads **Deadline missed** if the jar is short on it (rule 20). Money put into a jar does not lower the month's plan (rule 14); skipped months (rule 60) and later months' amounts typed by hand (O3b) are kept, while a figure typed for the current month is recalculated, as any other replan does (P29). Goals are unchanged. A payment dated ahead is counted once its date arrives and the next change or month start replans the jar; it does not replan on that day by itself (logged follow-up). Decided by Christopher on 27 October 2026 ("Recalculate on every payment").
